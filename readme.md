# [RL+Interference] Collaborative Interference Intelligent Decision Method Based on Hierarchical Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning [with code]

Introduction With the rapid development of electronic warfare technology, cognitive electronic warfare, integrating artificial intelligence, is gradually transitioning towards intelligent and networked characteristics. It has become a primary form of modern electronic warfare [1] and [3]. Cognitive electronic warfare primarily includes cognitive radar countermeasures and cognitive communication countermeasures [4] and [5]. It focuses on autonomous interaction environment learning and dynamic intelligent countermeasures. Intelligent interference decision-making is the core of cognitive countermeasure systems. It can dynamically devise the best interference strategy based on battlefield conditions. In situations where interference resources are limited, it can effectively improve the success rate of interference. Currently, experts and scholars mostly adopt reinforcement learning (RL)-related methods to propose some effective intelligent interference decision-making technologies [7]. This method can provide commanders with swift and accurate decision support in a timely manner, even applicable to unmanned aerial vehicle combat platforms. RL is a type of machine learning theory that does not require prior knowledge. The agent trains through interaction with the environment to maximize numerical values. Compared to traditional methods based on template matching [8], game theory [9], and inference [10], it further enhances the flexibility and real-time performance of the method. It is more conducive to solving decision-making problems in multidimensional, large space, and small sample scenarios under battlefield conditions.
Zhang et al. studied a multifunctional radar cognitive interference decision system [12]. They analyzed the fundamental principles of RL and DRL and their applications in cognitive interference decision systems. Zhuangsun et al. investigated apprentice learning under cognitive interference. Simulation results show that, in some known electronic warfare environments, apprentice learning is feasible and realistic for interference strategy [13]. Tian et al. proposed a DQN-based cognitive radar countermeasure method for optimizing target tracking performance in scenarios with multiple signals coexisting [14]. Experiments prove that RL has strong autonomy and adaptability, capable of solving such problems. However, the research mainly focuses on changes in the enemy radar's operational status. Specifically, by starting from the interference style, an adversarial model between the radar and the interferer is established to seek the optimal strategy. Interference decisionmaking is not only physical layer but can effectively leverage the advantages of intelligent interference through comprehensive decision-making from multiple dimensions.
Frequency domain, as an important signal feature domain, is an important entry point for radar interference. Waveform design is an important foothold for interference combat [15], [16]. Especially with the emergence of frequency jittering, frequency diversity, and other radar anti-interference technologies. On one hand, frequency jittering adopts pulse-to-pulse wideband hopping, which is extremely fast and difficult to be tracked by jammers [17]; on the other hand, frequency diversity allows signals to be transmitted on different carrier frequencies due to different radiation frequencies [18]. Therefore, narrowband frequency targeting jamming and deception forwarding jamming are difficult to quickly track, while broadband blocking jamming cannot achieve good interference effects due to the reduction of interference power spectral density [19]. How to optimize the allocation of interference resources to achieve the best interference effect with fewer interference forces and save interference signal transmission power without excessively occupying electromagnetic spectrum resources is a pressing issue. The study of intelligent frequency domain interference resource decision under radar frequency domain anti-interference measures is of great significance.
In addressing the problem of intelligent decision-making in frequency domain parameters, the battlefield conditions involve numerous targets with complex and variable frequency distributions. As the number of solutions grows exponentially, the computational load becomes unbearable. In the face of solving a large number of target problems, traditional reinforcement learning significantly reduces decision efficiency by querying Q-tables, which is not conducive to the actual needs of the battlefield environment. Deep reinforcement learning, in scenarios where little or no prior knowledge exists, provides optimal decision outcomes through interactive learning. It outputs action plans under the fitting of neural networks. Moreover, the determined perturbation actions have more than one parameter, combining multiple parameters as the output of an action strategy. As the number of perturbation actions increases, the advantages of deep reinforcement learning become more prominent in terms of efficiency.
Li et al. proposed a frequency decision algorithm based on hierarchical deep reinforcement learning (HDRL) to combat interference [20]. This algorithm determines communication frequencies according to the principles of hierarchical reinforcement learning. The advantage of this method is that it can avoid interference from unknown interference patterns. Kulkari et al. introduced an interference resource allocation decision algorithm based on Bootstrapping expert trajectories and HRL [21]. This method can achieve optimal interference effects by minimizing the interference bandwidth according to the priority order of interference targets. Zhang et al. applied the ideas of Markovian game framework to the problem of multi-user interference channel access, and proposed a multi-user collaborative interference channel selection algorithm based on RL, which can obtain the optimal anti-interference strategy. Wang et al. proposed a collaborative distributed computing and analysis mechanism based on Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MADRL) [23]. Simulation results show that the introduction of MADRL reduces the time delay of single-hop data packets in the network and improves system performance and global network resource allocation fairness. Xu et al. introduced the value decomposition-based Markovian reinforcement learning (MARL) into multi-task and multi-agent environments to solve the vehicle application problem in intelligent transportation systems [24].
The main contribution of this paper can be summarized as follows:
· A multi-agent collaborative frequency domain decision method based on hierarchical reinforcement learning is proposed. The global frequency domain interference task is decomposed into multiple subtasks, effectively solving the interference decision problem in the large interference action space and radar state space, improving interference efficiency.
· A frequency domain multi-agent interference resource allocation method based on PER-DDQN is proposed. By prioritizing experience replay, the interference decision process of agents is optimized. Compared with DQN and DDQN, this algorithm has significantly improved in action estimation and convergence speed.

Conclusion: Under the constraint of limited interference resources, priority is given to interfering with targets with higher threat levels, using as few interference resources as possible. A collaborative interference intelligent decision-making method based on hierarchical multi-agent reinforcement learning is proposed. A model for Multi-Agent MDP based on a centralized learning paradigm, known as FD-CJDM, is constructed. Following the ideas from HRL, the problem of dimensionality disaster in decision systems is effectively resolved. To accelerate the convergence speed of the algorithm, an optimized version of the PER DDQN algorithm using a tree structure is employed. Theoretically, the proposed interference intelligent decision-making method can be applied to almost all resource allocation problems for interference agents concerning frequency and bandwidth parameters, whether it involves single resource allocation or centralized multi-resource allocation, whether targeting a single radar system or multiple target radar network. This method avoids complex large action spaces through optimization, demonstrating strong applicability.
